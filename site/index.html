Social Capital publishes nine launches. Seven shipped with a video. I measured all seven and found no house style inside the frame — one is a single unbroken 140-second take, another cuts fifty times. But the frame itself barely moves, and the 2026 launches sit in a far tighter band than the 2025 ones. The format looks learned, not imposed.

n = 7 · no significance claimed

What the frame holds constant

Every launch video is landscape, carries a real audio track into a feed that autoplays muted, and runs between one and three minutes — several times the length short-form orthodoxy prescribes. Five of seven are cut at 23.976 fps, the cinema standard. Phones capture at 30 or 60; a 24p timeline is a decision somebody made.

Predictions, and how they closed

Nine hypotheses were written and committed to git before the measurement code existed. Three were formed after a scouting probe and are labelled confirmatory — they are not evidence of successful prediction. The rest were blind. Two of those failed, and the failures are the most informative results here.

What this does not claim

  • No causal claim. Nothing here says format drives reach. There is no control group and no counterfactual, so the honest statement is that these are the properties of the videos, not the reason they travelled.
  • No amplifier reconstruction. Who reposts each launch is the most valuable question and it sits behind the paid X API. Anything I produced without it would be guesswork wearing the costume of measurement.
  • No LinkedIn measurement. LinkedIn answers unauthenticated requests with HTTP 999. Half the distribution is therefore invisible to this analysis.
  • n = 7. Seven videos cannot support a significance test, and no p-value appears anywhere in this project. Everything above is descriptive.
  • Scene detection is a proxy for cutting. A slow dissolve reads as no cut; a hard lighting change can read as one. Counts are reported at three thresholds instead of one so the reader can see how much that matters.
